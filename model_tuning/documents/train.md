Here are the concepts, then what the rewritten script does about each.

1. Coverage, not just volume. With 16 node labels and 21 relationships, the risk isn't dataset size — it's that 80% of your pairs hit Patient/Charge/Visit while PhoneBridge, DiallerCall, or Campaign get five examples each. The model then confidently hallucinates properties on the labels it barely saw. The rule of thumb: every label and every relationship needs a minimum floor of examples (I default to 30 per label as a warning threshold; 50–100 is safer), weighted beyond that by how often real users actually ask about each entity. Your benchmark questions tell you the real distribution: patients, balances, locations, trends dominate — so those should be overrepresented, but never at the cost of zero coverage elsewhere.

2. The unit of splitting is the template, not the row. This is the concept most people get wrong. Synthetic datasets are usually paraphrase-heavy: "Monthly charge trend", "charge trend by month", "show charges per month" all map to nearly identical Cypher. If you shuffle rows and split, the val set is full of paraphrases of training rows — val loss looks great, real generalization is untested. The fix is group-aware splitting: compute a structural signature of each Cypher (strip literals, numbers, variable names), group rows by signature, and send the whole group to one split. Val then measures whether the model handles unseen intents, not unseen wordings.

3. Stratify so val mirrors the schema. A random 10% val slice from a skewed dataset can end up with zero BirdeyeReview or IVR questions — then "eval_loss 0.3" tells you nothing about a sixth of your schema. Stratifying by a derived category (primary_label : query_shape) forces each split to contain proportional slices of every label × shape combination.

4. Tag pairs automatically from the Cypher itself. You don't need hand-labeled categories. The query already encodes everything: which labels ((p:Patient) regex), which relationships ([:HAD_VISIT]), hop count, aggregation, time grouping. From those you derive difficulty (0-hop lookup → simple; 2-hop + time-grouped aggregate → hard) and category. This also gives you a free schema validation gate: any pair whose Cypher references a label or relationship not in the Modelfile schema is bad synthetic data and gets dropped before training.

5. Dedup before splitting, never after. Exact and normalized-question duplicates (case, punctuation, whitespace) must go first, or identical rows land in both train and val.

The rewritten script implements all five, plus it fixes a real bug in your v1: the stratify branch concatenated reset-index frames with df.loc[X_train.index, ...] lookups — after reset_index(drop=True) those indices no longer correspond, so questions were silently paired with the wrong Cypher. That alone could have poisoned any stratified run.

Practical targets for your schema: aim for roughly 3,000–8,000 unique templates total. Distribution guide — ~40% on the high-traffic entities and shapes your benchmark shows (Patient balances, trends, location rollups), ~30% multi-hop paths (Patient→Visit→Location, Charge→Diagnosis, the phone-bridge→campaign chain, since wrong hops are your #1 production failure), ~20% on the long-tail labels to hit the per-label floor, ~10% edge cases (year filters, quarters, string-date substring, NOT patterns). Run the script's coverage report first — it will tell you exactly which labels and relationships are starving before you spend a training run finding out.
